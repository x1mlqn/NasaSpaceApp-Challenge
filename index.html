ABOUT THE MISSION

A window onto the South Pole

This experience explores Sun and Earth visibility at the lunar South Pole. DE421 time series are recalculated for the selected point; the horizon profile comes from LOLA terrain.

3D TERRAINLOLA LDEM_75S_30M source: 30,496 × 30,496 signed samples, 30 m/px at the pole. Lossless tiled loading with dynamic detail around the observer.
ASTRONOMYJPL DE421 and DE421 lunar frames · one calculation per UTC hour.
CATALOGUSGS Gazetteer · named sites south of 80°S; coordinate search covers 90°S–80°S.
Tiles preserve 16-bit LOLA elevations (0.5 m per source step). Horizons are sampled every 30 m out to 100 km. The global mesh remains simplified; the 12 km near patch uses 30 m vertex spacing. Direct illumination is relative to an unobstructed Sun at normal incidence: clear solar disk fraction × cosine of incidence on the local slope. The center of Earth must clear the LOLA horizon for the link proxy; local areas also receive Three.js shadows. Period: November 14 to December 14, 2026.
